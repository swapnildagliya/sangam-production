ABC Dance Company  ·  November 2026  ·  Creative Direction

SANGAM

संगम  ·  Confluence

An all-female village. A river goddess. A rejected boy.
A calamity born from heartbreak. A queen whose love cost her everything.

Creative North Star

I

Ritual

Movement is meditative before it is explosive. Repetition, collective breathing, ensemble synchronisation. Stillness carries as much weight as the most complex choreography.

II

Spectacle

Strategic projections, fabric as landscape, lighting transformations. The blue dupatta is not a prop — it is the river. When it is pulled away, the audience watches a world die.

III

Emotional Arc

Every climactic moment must be earned through restraint. Joy is most devastating when someone is missing from it. The queen's sacrifice — her life — means nothing to an audience that has not watched her prison become her love.

Five Key Moments

SCENE 01 · BIRTH

What exists now

Opening Act

Direct narrative of birth and banishment. Blue dupatta as the river. A scream. Ensemble: Svetlana, Sara, Narcisse, Kaushika, Maha, Siddhy, Haike.

Creative Direction

Begin in total darkness. No music — only the sound of collective breath. The ensemble as one organism, one heartbeat. A single pool of blue light. Then — the Queen separates. She moves alone while others are still. The birth is not performed; it is expelled from the group. The baby passes through the ensemble on the blue dupatta as if carried by the river itself.

"The ritual must be established before it can be broken."
SCENE 11 · REJECTION

What exists now

The Queen's Rejection

The queen sees the boy at the feast and rejects him. Kalbeliya is at full energy. Narration follows.

Creative Direction

Build the Kalbeliya to its absolute peak. Then: the music cuts dead. Not a fade — a cut. In absolute stillness, the Queen makes one gesture. No music. No words. The boy doesn't react with rage. He simply understands. The audience should feel the violence of silence.

"Stillness, at the right moment, is the most devastating weapon."
SCENE 13 · DROUGHT

What exists now

Chaos & Drought

The river disappears. Dead trees. Strobe, smoke machine. Blue dupattas vanish.

Creative Direction

The blue dupattas must be physically taken — violently. Multiple dancers seize them and drag them offstage in a kinetic storm. The audience watches the river die. The ensemble becomes fragmented, searching. The collective breath of Scene 1 is shattered. Uma collapses, unseen.

"The audience must feel the absence of what was always there."
SCENE 21 · SACRIFICE

What exists now

The Queen's Sacrifice

The Queen realises her mistake and gives away her soul. Song: TBC. Swapnil & Svetlana.

Creative Direction

Strip everything. No ensemble. Perhaps no music — only breathing or wind. Her movement becomes smaller, lower — then inward. She does not fall. She gives. He reaches toward her; she does not reach back. She places her hand against his chest — and the lights shift before any music does. The audience understands what has happened before the village does. She is not lost. She chose where to go.

"She does not surrender. She gives everything."
S22–24 · RETURN

What exists now

Rainfall, Return & Holi

Ghanan Ghanan — rainfall. Ghar More Pardesiya — the boy is welcomed back. Holi celebration. Maha returns as River Goddess. Full company.

Creative Direction

Silence. Total blackout. One dancer. One drop. Then another. Then a monsoon. Blue dupattas re-enter one by one. Maha emerges from the centre — the River Goddess returning to her source. The boy is received, celebrated, loved. By the time Ghanan Ghanan hits full force, every body is water. The Holi finale is devastatingly joyful — but the queen's place is empty. The village celebrates. The boy celebrates. And the person who made it all possible is not there. The audience must feel that absence even as they are swept into the joy.

"Joy is most devastating when someone is missing from it."

Cast of SANGAM

Five named characters carrying the dramatic arc of the show.

K

The Queen

Rani Kanō

रानी कनो  ·  Queen of the village

Played by Svetlana Bubnova

Arc

Rigid and fearful → catalyst for catastrophe → redeemed through ultimate sacrifice. She acts from terror, not malice. Control is her religion and her prison — until she dissolves both, offering her life to send her soul into the boy's heart. She does not live to see the world she heals.

Movement Vocabulary

Imperial, grounded, commanding. Early scenes: stillness that demands attention. As she unravels: increasingly desperate, frantic. Scene 21: bare, still — then, in the act of giving, she moves inward. The final gesture is not collapse. It is release. Her last movement is the quietest thing on the stage.

Costume Direction

Royal and structured. Deep indigo, black, burgundy. Heavy jewellery. By the sacrifice scene, layers removed — human fragility beneath.

M

The Boy

Manu / Kavi

मनु / कवि  ·  The poet, the bridge

Played by Swapnil Dagliya

Arc

Hidden, innocent → gradually discovered and accepted → publicly rejected, heartbroken → transformative bridge to the village's healing. Seeks connection, not validation.

Movement Vocabulary

Gentle, fluid, responsive. Childhood: playful and innocent. Adult: quiet longing. Rejection: collapse or stillness — never rage. Appeal (S20): completely exposed, dancing with his entire being.

Costume Direction

Plain, natural materials. Simple clothing as a child. Contemporary, unstructured as an adult. By the appeal scenes — minimal, emphasising vulnerability.

P

The Bird-Guardians

Prabha & Chaaya

प्रभा & छाया  ·  Light & Shadow

Played by Shreya Vaidya & Laurien De Ridder

Arc

Cosmic caretakers who protect the boy not through ownership but through unconditional presence. They do not own him — they shelter him. Their role is freedom, not possession.

Movement Vocabulary

Flowing, aerial, bird-like. Veils suggest wings and flight. Near-synchronisation — two birds responding to the same wind. Rooted in Bollyfolk but with an ethereal, otherworldly quality.

Costume Direction

Flowing fabric suggesting wings. Light colours — ivory, pale gold, soft grey. Movement should do the work, not the costume.

V

The Messenger

Vayu

वायु  ·  The Wind

Played by Narcisse Merlier

Arc

Neutral observer and active messenger between worlds. Carries the village's plea to the Four Tribes. Neither judge nor saviour — the medium through which cosmic forces communicate.

Movement Vocabulary

Fluid, directional, purposeful. The wind doesn't hesitate. Movement has clear intention and direction. Unlike the birds (ethereal) or the queen (grounded), Vayu is always in transit.

Costume Direction

Contemporary and neutral — distinct from the village aesthetic. Could suggest both masculinity and androgyny. The wind has no fixed form.

S14 Tribe InvitationS19 Appeal
U

The River Goddess / Mother

Uma

उमा  ·  The source of all water

Played by Maha

Arc

Loving mother in human form → Goddess in crisis → her tears literally become the drought → divine blessing and restoration. Her emotion IS the cosmic force. She does not choose the drought — her heartbreak manifests it.

Movement Vocabulary

Grounded, maternal, fluid as water. Early scenes: warmth and earth. Drought: collapse without theatre — she simply stops flowing. Finale (S22–23): she returns not with drama but with inevitability. Water always finds its level.

Costume Direction

Blue silks and water-like fabrics throughout. In the final revelation — deep river blue with gold. She is the blue dupatta; when it is taken away, so is she.

Where SANGAM Comes From

The original concept, choreography inspirations, and visual references that shaped the production.

Origin Concept — April 2026

PURNA

पूर्ण  ·  Whole, Complete  ·  The concept that became SANGAM

In a distant land, there exists a village where no men have ever been born — a place where women and nature coexist in perfect rhythm, guided by the balance between the world and the waters.

One day, a woman becomes pregnant. When her child is born, the village gasps — it's a boy. His arrival disturbs the sacred harmony; rivers rage, skies dim, and the heartbeat of the earth falters.

Two celestial beings — the Sun and the Moon, appearing as radiant birds — take the boy under their care. They raise him with the wisdom of light and shadow, teaching him the dance of opposites: feminine & masculine.

The village queen watches silently. In the boy's existence, she senses both the chaos and the key to balance. She realises that only love — the most selfless form of creation — can restore the world's rhythm.

In an act of ultimate sacrifice, she offers her own life, sending her soul into the boy's heart. Through her love, the waters settle, the world breathes again, and harmony is reborn.

The village embraces the boy, no longer as a disruption, but as the embodiment of wholeness — of Purna, the eternal circle of love, sacrifice, and unity.

Each reference informs a specific scene, texture, or movement quality in SANGAM.

Beginning Sequence

ganavya — ami pana so'dras

The opening ritual quality. Tamil devotional music meeting contemporary minimalism — intimacy, stillness, the sacred before it becomes spectacle. Directly informs Scene 01's collective breath and the pre-verbal atmosphere before any movement begins.

LUMINI
A Contemporary Dance Piece

Choreography Inspiration

Lumini (A Contemporary Dance Piece)

Light as character. How darkness and emergence work together — bodies appearing from nothing, disappearing into nothing. Directly relevant to Scene 13 (drought blackout) and Scene 22 (the rainfall's first drop in total dark).

Aayirathil Oruvan
Celebration Of Life

Choreography Inspiration

Aayirathil Oruvan — Celebration Of Life

The tribal energy, scale, and visceral joy of the Holi finale. How Indian folk choreography commands a large stage with collective rhythm and colour. Direct reference for Scenes 17–18 (Bhangra & Bollyfolk tribes) and S24. The joy that exists even in the queen's absence.

Stages of Grief
AVANTGARDE SHOW 2023

Choreography Inspiration

Stages of Grief — AVANTGARDE SHOW 2023

Collective suffering staged physically — how grief moves through an ensemble as fragmentation, isolation, searching. Reference for Scene 13 (the ensemble breaks apart as the river dies) and Scene 19 (the village's appeal, searching together for something lost).

The Final Stage
Girar O Mundo

Choreography Inspiration

The Final Stage — Girar O Mundo

The language of sacrifice and release. What a body looks like when it gives itself completely — not collapse, but dissolution. Core reference for Scene 21. The queen does not fall. She moves inward. Her last gesture is the quietest thing on the stage.

Many Angels
Lar Lubovitch

Choreography Inspiration

Many Angels by Lar Lubovitch

The original Purna vision for Prabha & Chaaya: the Sun and Moon as guardians. Aerial, flowing duet work that expresses unconditional presence — sheltering without possessing. Direct inspiration for Scenes 02–03 and the birds' movement vocabulary throughout.

Max Richter
The Four Seasons

Choreography Inspiration

Max Richter — The Four Seasons (Dance Video)

Structural approach to the production arc — how seasonal and cyclical change maps onto bodies. Classical structure carrying contemporary movement. Relevant to the overall rhythm of 24 scenes and the idea of nature as the dominant character driving the human story.
